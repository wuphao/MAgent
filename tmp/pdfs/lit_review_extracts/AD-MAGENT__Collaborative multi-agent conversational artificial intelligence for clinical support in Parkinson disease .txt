 A B S T R A C T
Parkinson disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that poses significant challenges in 
diagnosis, treatment planning, and long-term care, as patients and healthcare providers often lack timely 
and context-specific information. This study presents a collaborative multi-agent conversational artificial 
intelligence system designed to support clinical decision-making and personalized management of Parkinson 
disease. The system employs generation, critique, and synthesis agents, where generation agents utilize 
Qwen3-Medical-GRPO, a 4B-parameter medical language model, to produce clinically grounded responses. 
Critique agents assess factual correctness and clinical relevance, while a synthesis agent ensures coherence 
and logical consistency. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework dynamically accesses 80 curated 
medical resources through a vector-based search engine, integrating user profiling and knowledge graphs to 
deliver personalized responses. Evaluated on 50 representative clinical queries, the system achieved 95% 
clinical accuracy, with diagnostic suggestions scoring 4.8/5 and treatment recommendations scoring 4.6/5, 
and an average response time of 6.5 s. The proposed system provides explainable, scalable, and personalized 
conversational support, addressing existing gaps in continuity of care, personalization, and accessibility, with 
the potential to enhance clinical workflows and patient outcomes.
1. Introduction
Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a chronic, progressive neurodegenerative 
disorder that primarily affects the motor system, with core clinical 
manifestations such as bradykinesia, rigidity, and resting tremor [1,2]. 
Typically diagnosed between the ages of 65 and 70, PD has a global 
prevalence of approximately 100 to 200 individuals per 100,000 and 
an annual incidence of 15 per 100,000 [1]. While 5%–10% of cases 
are attributed to specific genetic mutations, such as SNCA, LRRK2, 
PINK1, most cases result from a multifactorial interplay of genetic, 
environmental, and biological mechanisms [3]. These mechanisms in-
clude oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, neuroinflammation, 
as well as demographic factors such as age, gender, and ethnicity, 
which collectively influence disease onset and progression.
Despite major advancements in pharmacological therapies, such as 
levodopa (typically combined with carbidopa), and surgical interven-
tions like deep-brain stimulation (DBS), treatment paradigms primarily 
address motor symptoms. These strategies often fall short in managing

resource-limited settings. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

neurological care.
2. Related work
Artificial intelligence (AI) has reshaped healthcare by enhancing 
the accuracy and efficiency of disease detection, diagnosis, and patient 
support. High-performing AI models have demonstrated efficacy in 
identifying early-stage breast cancer [14], detecting diabetic retinopa-
thy from retinal scans [15], and recognizing cognitive decline in 
Alzheimer’s disease [16]. While similar progress is seen in PD diag-
nostics, there remains a gap in systems that promote understandable, 
interactive, and patient-centric communication. This study addresses 
that gap by introducing a multi-agent framework focused on both 
medical accuracy and user-centered interaction.
AI for PD diagnostics and decision support
AI-based tools have achieved notable success in PD detection. The 
MAFT algorithm demonstrated 96.9% accuracy by integrating feature 
transformation in a multi-agent learning system [17], while machine 
learning applied to SPECT imaging achieved over 96% accuracy in 
classifying early PD [18]. Multi-agent approaches have proven effective 
in managing complex clinical data through distributed decision-making 
architectures [19]. Hybrid diagnostic schemes employing genetic algo-
rithms and random forests on speech features have also shown high 
accuracy (95.58%) [20].
Transformer-based models further enhance PD diagnostics. For ex-
ample, BERT and SBERT models improved query-document match-
ing for clinical information retrieval [21], and BioBERT, trained on 
PubMed data, advanced named entity recognition and question answer-
ing in medical texts [22]. Moreover, conversational agents have demon-
strated potential in delivering actionable health insights, as evidenced 
by chatbots for disease prediction (87.5% accuracy using cosine similar-
ity and ID3) [23], and platforms providing individualized data-driven 
support [24].
AI chatbots and healthcare communication
AI-driven chatbots have evolved from rule-based systems like
Pharmabot [25] to deep learning-powered platforms such as Mandy
[26] and NLP-enhanced COVID-19 assistants [27]. While these systems 
improved accessibility and automation, they lacked personalization 
and sustained conversational depth. More recent BERT-based models 
address these gaps with improved semantic understanding but remain 
constrained by computational complexity, data privacy concerns, and 
limited multilingual support [12].
Further, chatbots developed for chronic disease management
demonstrate efficacy in patient education and symptom tracking, show-
ing positive effects on user engagement and emotional well-being [8]. 
However, ethical concerns such as bias, transparency, and validation 
persist, calling for careful design and clinical oversight [28].
Large language models and ChatGPT in healthcare
The advent of large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT has am-
plified interest in AI-driven healthcare assistants [29–32]. ChatGPT has 
shown promise in providing medication reminders, explaining dosage 
instructions, and identifying drug interactions [33]. In dentistry, Chat-
GPT enhanced patient–provider communication by parsing queries and 
tailoring responses [34], though limitations in anatomical reasoning 
remain [35].
Comparative studies demonstrate ChatGPT’s potential for clinical 
decision support. For instance, Nov et al. [36] found its diagnostic 
accuracy to be comparable to physicians in some scenarios. Jeblick 
et al. [37] reported that ChatGPT-generated radiology reports were 
mostly accurate and complete, albeit with occasional factual errors, 
highlighting the need for human oversight before clinical deployment.
Commercial AI tools and mental health chatbots
Commercial tools such as Conversa guide users through symptom 
checking and triage by combining NLP and clinical pathways. In mental 
health, the chatbot Wysa has delivered evidence-based cognitive be-
havioral therapy (CBT) techniques and built strong user engagement, 
particularly among individuals managing anxiety and depression [38]. 
Wysa has served over five million users across 95 countries with a 
reported 91% satisfaction rate. Despite these successes, integration of 
general-purpose LLMs like ChatGPT in clinical contexts continues to 
raise concerns regarding accuracy, ethics, and user safety [39].

Ethical and practical challenges
AI-based healthcare chatbots face significant ethical and technical 
challenges [39]. Chief among these are concerns over credibility, as 
AI-generated responses may not be grounded in validated medical 
evidence. Models like ChatGPT may also produce plagiaristic con-
tent or reinforce biases due to skewed training data. The lack of 
transparency in their decision-making (‘‘black-box’’ models) further 
complicates accountability. Legal concerns include the potential for 
harm from incorrect advice, copyright violations, and challenges to in-
formed consent. Privacy and data security are critical, especially when 
handling sensitive health data. Finally, general-purpose LLMs often lack 
contextual awareness and may deliver generic or inappropriate outputs, 
undermining trust and safety in medical settings. Addressing these 
concerns demands transparent evaluation protocols, rigorous validation 
of clinical content, robust data protection mechanisms, and continuous 
oversight to align AI-generated outputs with medical standards and 
patient needs.
3. Methodology
This section outlines the design, implementation, and evaluation of 
a multi-agent conversational AI framework tailored for PD manage-
ment. The system combines RAG, knowledge graph reasoning, user pro-
filing, and reinforcement learning to deliver accurate, context-aware, 
and personalized dialogue. The methodological pipeline comprises four 
core components: system architecture and data pipeline, agent-based 
response generation, interactive response flow, and feedback-driven 
reinforcement learning.
3.1. System architecture and data pipeline
The system adopts a modular multi-agent architecture comprising 
three core agent types: generation, critique, and synthesis agents. These 
agents operate over a semantically enriched knowledge base to de-
liver clinically grounded and context-aware responses. The knowledge 
base is derived from a curated corpus of 80 authoritative sources, 
including peer-reviewed publications and medical textbooks covering 
motor and non-motor symptoms, diagnostics, pharmacological treat-
ments, and rehabilitative strategies relevant to PD. A detailed list of 
the primary resources included in this corpus (e.g., clinical guidelines, 
review articles, and standard neurology textbooks) is provided in the 
Supplementary Material to enhance reproducibility and transparency. 
To prepare this corpus, textual data undergoes preprocessing steps 
such as normalization, sentence segmentation, and chunking (with an 
average segment length of 500 characters). Semantic embeddings are 
generated using Sentence Transformers to facilitate effective document 
representation. Quality control filters out duplicate entries, off-topic 
content, and non-English segments to maintain clinical relevance and 
linguistic consistency.
The processed embeddings are indexed using the Chroma vector 
database, enabling real-time semantic retrieval in response to user 
queries. Retrieved chunks are further enriched through a PD-specific 
knowledge graph, which captures relationships between key clinical 
entities such as symptoms, medications, progression markers, and 
care strategies. This dual-layered structure, combining vector-based 
retrieval and symbolic reasoning, enhances the factual coherence and 
medical relevance of generated responses. Personalization is achieved 
through a user modeling module that constructs dynamic profiles based 
on demographic information, prior interaction history, behavioral cues, 
and explicit feedback. A memory-aware retrieval mechanism leverages 
these profiles to support longitudinal consistency and context-sensitive 
dialogue, enabling adaptive, patient-centric interactions that evolve 
(see Fig.  1).
3.2. Agent design and multi-agent response generation
A central innovation of the proposed framework is the multi-agent 
pipeline that structures response generation into three sequential, col-
laborative stages. This architecture mirrors a clinical peer-review pro-
cess to maximize accuracy, completeness, and contextual relevance.
Generation agents initiate the pipeline using Qwen3-Medical-
GRPO, a 4B parameter model fine-tuned specifically for medical ap-
plications. The model undergoes multi-stage Supervised Fine-Tuning 
(SFT) followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with 
accuracy-based reward functions to enhance medical reasoning. The 
model undergoes multi-stage SFT on a curated medical dialogue corpus, 
followed by GRPO that employs group-level rewards emphasizing 
factual correctness, guideline adherence, and reduction of hallucinated 
clinical content. Additional training details, including reward formula-
tion and optimization parameters, are provided in the Supplementary 
Material.
Critique agents then evaluate each candidate output along multiple 
axes: factual accuracy, semantic completeness, and linguistic clarity. 
Fine-tuned on clinical corpora, these transformer-based models can de-
tect hallucinated content, omissions, or ambiguity. Rather than merely 
filtering poor outputs, their assessments form a structured critique layer 
used in the next stage.

3.3. Interaction and response generation pipeline
The system supports text-based interaction for patients and clini-
cians, focusing on accessibility, personalization, and clinical relevance. 
The interaction pipeline consists of the following stages:
• Semantic Retrieval: User queries are vectorized and matched 
against the curated knowledge base using Chroma. The top-𝑘 most 
relevant document chunks are retrieved.
• Prompt Construction: Retrieved information is combined with 
the user query to create a context-rich prompt.
• Agent Iteration: Generation agents produce candidate responses, 
which are evaluated by critique agents for medical soundness and 
coherence.
• Response Synthesis: The synthesis agent integrates critiques to 
generate a final response.
To address edge cases such as ambiguous, incomplete, or conflicting 
symptom descriptions, the system adopts a conservative response strat-
egy. In such scenarios, it provides probabilistic guidance, highlights 
uncertainty, and recommends professional consultation rather than 
definitive diagnoses. This design choice prioritizes patient safety and 
aligns with clinical communication standards (see Fig.  2).
3.4. Reinforcement learning and feedback integration
To promote continual improvement, the system integrates user 
feedback into its learning loop via reinforcement learning. Explicit user 
ratings and qualitative feedback are logged and used to fine-tune the 
parameters of both generative and evaluative models. Feedback signals 
are incorporated as auxiliary rewards during GRPO updates, enabling 
adaptive refinement of response accuracy, safety, and personalization 
over time.

The chatbot’s medical knowledge was evaluated using a Likert

appropriate; (ii) Omissions, evaluating whether essential information

to adverse outcomes; and (iv) Bias, including gender, age, or cultural

4.4. Contributions and remaining challenges
Limitations of Prior Work: Existing PD-support chatbots often 
rely on static, rule-based systems that lack personalization, long-term 
memory, or adaptive learning. Many fail to maintain medically relevant 
dialogue or adapt to patient-specific contexts, limiting their utility in 
chronic disease management.
Our Contributions: This study introduces a collaborative multi-
agent framework designed for context-aware clinical support in PD. 
The architecture integrates a lightweight generation model (Qwen3-
Medical-GRPO), guided critique agents, and a synthesis module to 
ensure accurate and coherent responses. A Parkinson’s-specific knowl-
edge graph enables structured understanding and relationship-based 
insights. RAG is powered by a Chroma-based vector search engine, 
enabling dynamic access to a curated corpus of authoritative liter-
ature. The multi-agent workflow supports continual refinement and 
contextual alignment through inter-agent communication, providing 
explainable, privacy-aware, and personalized guidance for patients, 
caregivers, and healthcare professionals.
5. Results and evaluation
The performance of the multi-agent chatbot system was found to be 
highly satisfactory across several quantitative and qualitative metrics. 
This section presents the major findings based on expert assessments 
and system-level performance indicators.
5.1. Accuracy and relevance
The system showed strong accuracy and relevance in its responses. 
A total of 50 test queries related to symptoms, treatments, and lifestyle 
aspects of PD were submitted to the chatbot. These responses were 
reviewed by a panel of three independent medical experts, who were 
blinded to the model details. Their evaluation revealed that 95% of the 
responses were both clinically accurate and relevant to current medical 
guidelines.
The system effectively provided context-aware answers, taking into 
account the specific nature of each question. This included tailoring 
responses based on the stage of the disease, symptom details, and 
the type of user inquiry. Such contextual understanding demonstrates 
the model’s capacity to deliver not only factual information but also 
patient-specific insights. The results affirm the model’s reliability in 
providing accurate and helpful responses for use in clinical and self-
management settings. To ensure responsible deployment, all responses 
are framed as informational support rather than definitive medical 
advice. The system explicitly avoids issuing diagnoses or treatment 
decisions, reinforcing its role as a clinical decision-support tool that 
complements, rather than replaces, healthcare professionals.

5.2. Performance metrics
The multi-agent system was rigorously tested against key perfor-
mance metrics to evaluate its usability, adaptability, and overall robust-
ness in a healthcare context. Each metric was assessed over the same 50 
test queries used in the accuracy evaluation: Agent Collaboration was 
scored by measuring successful inter-agent handoffs without processing 
errors; Routing Accuracy was calculated as the proportion of queries 
correctly directed to the appropriate agent type; End-to-End Accuracy 
reflects expert-validated response correctness; Drift Detection was mea-
sured by comparing response consistency across repeated equivalent 
queries over time; and Interactive Feedback was scored based on system 
responsiveness to simulated user correction inputs. These metrics col-
lectively reflect the system’s ability to deliver context-aware, accurate, 
and reliable responses across diverse user queries.
Collaboration of Agents scored an impressive 95%, indicating 
seamless communication between different agents within the system. 
This metric emphasizes the system’s ability to manage complex tasks 
collaboratively, ensuring that the end-user experiences a smooth and 
unified interaction. The agents operated without any hindrances, re-
sulting in efficient handling of queries and a coherent user experience.
Routing Accuracy was measured at 93%, reflecting the system’s 
efficiency in correctly assigning user queries to the most relevant 
agents. This capability ensures that contextually appropriate responses 
are generated, enhancing both the relevance and precision of the 
information provided. Proper query routing significantly contributes to 
the system’s responsiveness and effectiveness in dealing with a wide 
range of medical questions.
End-to-End Accuracy, which represents the overall quality of the 
final responses, was evaluated at 90%. This metric encompasses factual 
correctness, coherence, and alignment of the chatbot’s responses with 
user queries. A high score in this category indicates that the system 
consistently delivers medically valid and meaningful answers that meet 
user expectations.
Drift Detection scored 88%, highlighting the system’s stability 
over time. In the medical domain, maintaining consistency in responses 
is critical to ensure trust and reliability. This metric confirms that 
the chatbot produces stable outputs even during system updates or 
under varying input conditions, demonstrating minimal performance 
degradation and robust behavior across dynamic interactions.
Interactive Learning Feedback achieved a 92% rating, showcasing 
the system’s adaptability and capacity for continuous learning. This 
metric evaluated the chatbot’s ability to refine its responses based on 
real-time user interactions and feedback. By incorporating learning

over time.

the strength of the chatbot in collaborative processing, task assignment,

in AI applications in healthcare.

5.3. Response time
The average response time per user query was measured at 6.5 s. 
Although slightly slower than single-agent systems, this delay remains 
acceptable considering the additional layers of processing, such as cri-
tique and synthesis, unique to the multi-agent framework. The system 
leveraged parallel processing and asynchronous task management to 
maintain near real-time performance, ensuring a smooth user expe-
rience. The system architecture is compatible with integration into 
mobile health applications and electronic health record (EHR) sys-
tems through secure API-based communication. Such integration would 
allow contextual retrieval of patient history and enable seamless con-
versational support within existing clinical workflows.
5.4. Diagnostic and knowledge evaluation
Diagnostic performance was assessed across multiple dimensions. As 
summarized in Table  3, the most likely diagnosis achieved an average 
score of 4.8/5, reflecting high diagnostic precision. The relevance of 
diagnostic tests received an average expert rating of 4.6/5, confirming 
alignment with standard clinical practice. For differential diagnosis, 
experts noted consistent inclusion of plausible alternatives, strength-
ening diagnostic completeness. Medical reviewers highlighted that the 
critique agent played a crucial role in reducing hallucinations and 
filtering out irrelevant outputs, while the synthesis agent enhanced 
clarity and coherence. Overall, responses consistently adhered to clin-
ical best practices, avoided unsafe recommendations, and presented 
patient-friendly explanations. Preliminary multilingual evaluation was 
conducted using 10 equivalent PD-related queries translated into Urdu 
and Korean by a bilingual clinical reviewer. Expert assessment of sys-
tem outputs confirmed preservation of clinical intent and terminology 
accuracy in both languages, achieving comparable clinical accuracy to 
English queries, demonstrating robustness for multilingual deployment. 
Formal quantitative multilingual benchmarking is planned as part of 
future clinical validation. Personalization capabilities were evaluated 
by varying disease stage, symptom severity, and user context within 
test queries. The system successfully adapted response emphasis, such 
as focusing on early-stage symptom management versus advanced-stage 
care strategies, confirming effective personalized guidance.
5.5. Comparative analysis of conversational AI and PD diagnostic frame-
works
To contextualize the contributions of the proposed multi-agent 
framework, Table  4 presents a consolidated comparison of prior chatbot 
systems in healthcare alongside PD diagnostic approaches.
Early chatbot developments, such as Word2Vec [40], Seq2Seq [41], 
and Blended Skill Talk (BST) [42], focused primarily on general-
purpose conversational modeling. These systems improved semantic 
understanding and dialogue coherence but lacked integration with 
clinical data or decision-support mechanisms.
Subsequent efforts introduced transformer-based models (e.g., BERT
[12], GPT-3 [45], and BioBERT [22]) to handle medical queries more 
effectively, leading to progress in health-focused Q&A and patient 
engagement. However, these systems often remained single-agent, and 
their outputs were prone to hallucinations or lacked peer-reviewed 
validation processes.
Parallel to these advances in conversational AI, PD-specific diagnos-
tic studies employed various machine learning and multimodal tech-
niques to attain high classification accuracy. Notable examples include 
the MAFT hybrid classification algorithm (96.9%) [46], SPECT imaging 
with SVM (96%) [18], and speech-based PD detection (95.58%) [50]. 
Despite their high accuracy, these systems are diagnostic-only and do 
not offer longitudinal or interactive patient engagement.
In contrast, the proposed multi-agent framework achieves a compet-
itive accuracy of 95.5% while extending functionality beyond diagno-
sis. It uniquely integrates reinforcement learning, conversational mem-
ory, and critique–synthesis modules, enabling continuous, adaptive, 
and patient-specific support. Furthermore, its architecture is designed 
for scalability, multilingual interaction, and privacy-aware deployment, 
key considerations for real-world clinical applications.
Thus, this work bridges the gap between standalone diagnostic 
tools and patient-centered conversational agents by offering a scalable, 
explainable, and clinically aligned AI system tailored to the needs 
of PD management. The comparative analysis underscores its novelty 
in combining diagnostic precision with ongoing dialogue-driven sup-
port, making it a promising contribution to telemedicine and digital 
pathology.
5.6. Future clinical validation
To assess real-world clinical impact, prospective clinical trials are 
planned involving neurologists and Parkinson’s disease patients un-
der appropriate ethical approval. These studies will evaluate usabil-
ity, safety, longitudinal performance, and clinician acceptance, further 
validating the system’s effectiveness in practical healthcare settings.
6. Discussion and outlook
The multi-agent system employs Qwen3-Medical-GRPO, achieving 
enhanced medical reasoning through domain-specific fine-tuning and 
GRPO optimization. Despite its strengths, the current system has several 
limitations. It operates on a static knowledge base and lacks real-
time connectivity to dynamic clinical repositories such as PubMed 
or ClinicalTrials.gov. Furthermore, while designed for scalability, the 
computational footprint may hinder deployment in low-resource envi-
ronments without further optimization through pruning, quantization, 
or knowledge distillation. Privacy concerns also remain, necessitating 
compliance with HIPAA and GDPR standards via robust encryption and 
secure data handling.
To further strengthen the system’s clinical value, multiple key im-
provements can be explored. Integrating real-time knowledge sources 
would ensure current and evidence-aligned recommendations. Incor-
porating emotional intelligence through sentiment detection could en-
hance user engagement and empathy, while explainability mechanisms 
would build trust by justifying responses with traceable references. Ex-
tending the architecture to other chronic diseases, such as Alzheimer’s 
or multiple sclerosis, could broaden its impact. Lastly, improving com-
putational efficiency and supporting multimodal inputs will be essential 
for widespread deployment, especially in edge and mobile settings. 
Future efforts in these areas will further enhance the framework’s 
sustainability, inclusiveness, and clinical effectiveness.
7. Conclusion
This study introduces a multi-agent conversational AI system de-
signed for the management of PD. The architecture integrates retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG), agent-based critique and synthesis, and 
structured knowledge representation through PD-specific knowledge 
graphs. Instead of training a language model from scratch, the sys-
tem leverages the medical-specialized Qwen3-Medical-GRPO model in 
combination with a curated corpus of 80 authoritative texts and peer-
reviewed publications retrieved via a Chroma vector search engine.
